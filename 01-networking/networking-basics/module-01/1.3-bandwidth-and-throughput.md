# Bandwidth and Throughput

---

## Bandwidth vs. Throughput

| Concept | What It Means | Analogy |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandwidth** | Maximum theoretical speed of a connection | The width of a highway (how many lanes exist) |
| **Throughput** | Actual speed achieved in real life | The actual traffic flow (how many cars move when there is traffic) |
| **Latency** | Delay time for data to travel from source to destination | Time spent sitting at red lights along the way |

> **Security Note:** High latency or a sudden drop in throughput can indicate network congestion, misconfigurations, or potential Denial of Service (DoS) attacks saturating the connection.

---

## Units of Bandwidth

Bandwidth is measured in **bits per second (bps)**:

| Unit | Abbreviation | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| **Bits per second** | bps | Fundamental unit |
| **Kilobits per second** | Kbps | 1,000 bps |
| **Megabits per second** | Mbps | 1,000,000 bps |
| **Gigabits per second** | Gbps | 1,000,000,000 bps |
| **Terabits per second** | Tbps | 1,000,000,000,000 bps |

---

## Factors Affecting Real-World Speed

Throughput is almost always lower than bandwidth because of:

* **Traffic Volume:** Too much data moving across the wire at once.
* **Data Type:** Certain types of traffic require extra processing overhead.
* **Network Devices:** Every switch, router, and firewall in the path adds latency.

Tools like **Speedtest.net** measure real-world throughput (download and upload speeds) across your ISP connection.

---

## Quick Quiz Notes
- **Bandwidth:** The maximum capacity of a physical medium to carry data.
- **Throughput:** The actual transmission speed, which includes any delays or latency encountered.
- **Bits per second (bps):** The standard unit of measurement for data transmission rates.
