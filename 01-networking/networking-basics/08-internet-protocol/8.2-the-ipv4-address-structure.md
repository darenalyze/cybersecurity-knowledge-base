# The IPv4 Address Structure

## Networks and Hosts

An IPv4 address is **hierarchical** and made of **two parts**:

1. **Network Portion:** Identifies the specific network (like a street name). Must be **identical** for all devices on the same local network (LAN).
2. **Host Portion:** Identifies a specific device (like a house number). Must be **unique** for every device on that network.

### How Communication Works

* **Same LAN:** Devices with the **same network portion** can communicate directly.
* **Different LAN:** Devices with a **different network portion** cannot communicate without a **router**.
* **Misconfiguration Risk:** If a device is moved to a new network without updating its network portion, it will **lose connectivity**.

> **Security Note:** Network segmentation (dividing a network into smaller separate logical networks) limits the spread of malware and cyber threats across an organization.

---

### Visual Structure

```text
       IPv4 Address: 192 . 168 . 5 . 11
                     |___________|   ||
                           |         ||
                   Network Portion   Host Portion
                   (Must be SAME)    (Must be UNIQUE)
```

### Example: Department Networks
*(Example from Module)*

* **Network Management Dept:** `192.168.1.x`
* **Accounting Dept:** `192.168.2.x`
* **Sales Dept:** `192.168.3.x`
  * PC 1 in Sales: `192.168.3.10`
  * All devices in Sales share `192.168.3` (Network ID), but have unique ending numbers (Host ID).

### Example: Phone System Analogy
*(Example from Module)*

An IPv4 address works like a phone number:
* **Network Portion** = Country Code + Area Code + Exchange *(locates the region)*
* **Host Portion** = Local Subscriber Number *(locates the specific person)*
