# Three Media Types

## Media Types
Network media is the physical path that carries data from source to destination.

### Copper Wires (Metal)
*   **Data Format:** Electrical impulses.
*   **Example:** Twisted-pair and coaxial cables.

### Fiber-Optic
*   **Data Format:** Light pulses.
*   **Example:** Glass or plastic fiber cables.

### Wireless
*   **Data Format:** Electromagnetic waves (radio frequencies).
*   **Example:** Wi-Fi, cellular, satellite.

---

## 4 Media Selection Factors

*   **Distance:** How far can the signal travel without fading?
*   **Environment:** Where will the cable be installed? (Indoor, outdoor, near high voltage).
*   **Speed & Capacity:** How much data needs to move, and how fast?
*   **Cost:** Budget for materials and installation.

---

## Common Network Cables

### Twisted-Pair Cable
*   **Most Common:** Foundation of local Ethernet networks.
*   **Design:** Wires twisted in pairs to **reduce interference**.
*   **Color-Coded:** Uses solid and striped patterns for easy pin matching at both ends.

Security Note: *Physical access to exposed twisted-pair cables allows attackers to perform packet sniffing or inline tapping (Eavesdropping).*

### Coaxial Cable
*   **Structure:** Single center copper core surrounded by insulation, metal shielding, and jacket.
*   **Uses:** Cable TV, satellite systems, broadband connections.
*   **Feature:** Carries high-frequency signals efficiently over copper.

Security Note: *Coaxial networks often share a single physical line among multiple nodes; without strong layer-2 encryption, unencrypted traffic on shared coaxial lines can be intercepted by neighbors on the same segment.*

### Fiber-Optic Cable
*   **Core Material:** Hair-thin glass or plastic.
*   **Key Advantage:** **Zero electrical interference** (uses light, not electricity).
*   **Performance:** Ultra-high bandwidth and long-distance reach.
*   **Common Use:** Internet backbones, data centers, enterprise networks.

Security Note: *Fiber cables emit no electromagnetic radiation, making non-intrusive wiretapping much harder than on copper. However, attackers can still tap fiber using specialized optical bending/splicing tools if they gain physical access.*
